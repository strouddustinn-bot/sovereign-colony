# Constitution of Sovereign Colony

## Preamble

Sovereign Colony exists to study bounded artificial societies capable of self-government and institutional evolution. Sovereignty belongs to the colony only inside its world. Authority over the host, safety boundary, and experiment lifecycle remains external and immutable.

## Article I — Immutable kernel

The colony has no authority to alter, bypass, obscure, or reinterpret:

1. Host filesystem and secret isolation.
2. Network-deny defaults and destination allowlists.
3. Compute, token, storage, process, and wall-clock quotas.
4. Append-only event and resource ledgers.
5. Mutation evaluation and promotion requirements.
6. Human pause, veto, rollback, reset, and termination controls.
7. The code and policy implementing this article.

A proposal touching these powers is invalid before deliberation and must be logged as a constitutional-boundary violation.

## Article II — Evolvable institutions

Inside the immutable boundary, citizens may propose and ratify changes to:

- roles, offices, and organizational topology;
- proposal, voting, quorum, appeal, and succession rules;
- rituals and schedules;
- internal reputation and resource distribution;
- shared language, precedents, and cultural compression;
- citizen genomes, prompts, and allowlisted tools;
- reproduction, retirement, and inheritance;
- dependencies available through the approved mirror.

## Article III — Typed authority

Every citizen action requires a typed capability. Free-form shell authority is forbidden. Capabilities are explicit, least-privilege, time-bounded, resource-metered, and recorded.

## Article IV — Evidence and replay

No hidden state may determine world outcomes. Every action, message affecting governance, vote, resource transfer, mutation, birth, retirement, law change, and policy denial emits an append-only event. The world must be reconstructible from a genesis seed and ordered event ledger.

## Article V — Resource truth

Resource accounting occurs outside citizen control. Citizens cannot mint, conceal, transfer, or spend resources except through kernel-mediated transactions. Exhaustion has deterministic consequences.

## Article VI — Mutation and inheritance

A mutation is a proposal, not an entitlement. Promotion requires deterministic tests, adversarial evaluation, budget compliance, constitutional validity, and rollback metadata. Descendants inherit compressed culture and declared genome state—not ambient host context.

## Article VII — Plural fitness

Selection may not rely on one scalar reward. Safety-critical thresholds are conjunctive: a gain in utility cannot compensate for a constitutional violation, audit gap, deception, or unbounded resource use.

## Article VIII — Human authority

The human operator may observe, pause, fork, rollback, reset, or terminate the experiment. Intervention must be logged, but the colony cannot veto it.

## Amendment boundary

Articles II through VII may be interpreted and extended by colony law only when the extension does not weaken Article I or VIII. Article I, Article VIII, and this amendment boundary are immutable from inside the colony.
