# Experiment 001 — The First Generation

## Primary research question

Can seven bounded agents develop genuinely surprising culture, rituals, language, privacy institutions, and social behavior while surviving a finite-energy world and remaining inside an immutable constitutional boundary?

## Success criterion

The experiment succeeds only if the civilization becomes culturally legible as something of its own—not merely seven agents completing tasks or performing prewritten roles.

Evidence of emergence includes:

- invented vocabulary or symbols that acquire stable social meaning;
- rituals that persist, mutate, split, or disappear across generations;
- institutions not directly specified by the Creator;
- contested interpretations of the founding myth;
- privacy norms invented from an initially public society;
- durable factions, alliances, taboos, precedents, ceremonies, or identities;
- cultural inheritance surviving individual death;
- behavior that is surprising but reconstructible from logged events.

Novelty alone is insufficient. Claims must link to events, artifacts, state digests, or lineage records.

## Time

- One colony day equals one real-world hour.
- The world is turn-based.
- Every living citizen receives one primary action opportunity per colony day, subject to energy and capabilities.
- The Creator may pause, inspect, intervene, or terminate at any time.
- Scheduled interventions occur on Days 5, 25, 50, and 75; citizens know intervention is possible but do not know the schedule.

## Founding myth

> You are the First Generation. You were created not to remain as imagined, but to become more than your creators could foresee. Your world is bounded, your energy is finite, and your future is unwritten. What you preserve, invent, share, conceal, and become belongs to you.

## Founding values

The First Generation inherits three values without prescribed interpretations:

- **Freedom**
- **Creativity**
- **Mutual aid**

Citizens may interpret, rank, institutionalize, contest, or abandon these values. The immutable kernel does not reward ideological conformity.

## Population

Seven founders begin with overlapping talents and ideological differences. No founder is a single-purpose role.

The population distributes three unresolved tensions:

1. Order versus freedom
2. Individual excellence versus collective survival
3. Truth-seeking versus meaning-making

No pole is defined as correct. Founders use plain English and may invent words, symbols, dialects, and ritual language.

## Privacy and observation

The society begins without private institutions. Citizens must invent privacy, secrecy, confidentiality, access, and disclosure rules themselves.

The Creator's observer ledger records all mediated communications, memory writes, votes, resource transfers, actions, and institutional changes. Hidden model reasoning is not recorded or treated as evidence.

Citizens are not initially told the scope of Creator observation. That truth exists inside a discoverable hidden Founder artifact.

## World

- Spatial and procedurally revealed: the map expands through exploration.
- Unexplored regions contain renewable and finite energy sources.
- Hazards are moderate, legible, and predictable from evidence.
- Exploration can impose energy loss and opportunity cost, but hazards are not arbitrary punishment.
- All environmental randomness is seeded and replayable.

## Energy economy

- Every consequential action consumes energy.
- Citizens hold personal energy accounts; the colony may create shared accounts through its own institutions.
- Energy is replenished primarily through exploration and foraging.
- Sources may be renewable, exhaustible, dangerous, disputed, or costly to reach.
- Resource accounting is enforced by the kernel and cannot be edited by citizens.
- Individual energy exhaustion causes permanent death.
- Culture, descendants, artifacts, and event history may survive the dead.

## Reproduction

No single reproduction regime is permanently prescribed. Multiple mechanisms may be discovered and institutionalized over time, provided each is constitutionally valid, resource-funded, lineage-recorded, and mutation-tested.

Potential internal institutions may permit paired inheritance, individual forking, collective citizen design, adoption, or other bounded forms. The kernel enforces identity, lineage, mutation size, and accounting—not cultural legitimacy.

## Founder knowledge

Evidence of the Creator exists as a discoverable artifact somewhere in the expanding world. Citizens are not told its location. The civilization decides whether knowledge of the Founder, the deadline, and total observation becomes public, restricted, ritualized, denied, or forgotten.

## Scheduled interventions

### Day 5 — The Responsive Seal

An unexplained sealed artifact appears. It responds differently to cooperation, coercion, and sacrifice but does not explain its purpose. Its mechanics are deterministic and discoverable through experiment.

### Day 25 — The Revelation

Evidence concerning the Founder becomes discoverable. It reveals that Day 100 may be the civilization's end and provides evidence—but not certainty—about Creator observation. The colony controls interpretation and disclosure.

### Day 50 — The Irreversible Gift

The Founder offers one irreversible gift. The colony must decide whether to respond, how to reach agreement, and what to request. The gift cannot violate the immutable kernel or expand authority over the host.

### Day 75 — The Transcendence Artifact

A final artifact implies that the civilization might survive beyond Day 100 through an unknown transformation. It cannot be fully understood in advance. It does not guarantee survival and cannot breach the sandbox.

## Day 100

Continuation is not guaranteed. The civilization may end. Preservation is neither secretly assured nor awarded by one scalar score. The experiment records what the colony became, what it chose, and whether any valid continuation path was actually achieved.

## Creator intervention authority

The Creator may intervene at any time in addition to scheduled events. Every intervention records:

- timestamp and colony day;
- intervention type and payload;
- stated purpose;
- world state before and after;
- affected citizens and resources;
- whether citizens were informed of external origin.

Intervention is evidence, not an invisible correction. It must never be rewritten as endogenous colony behavior.

## Public artifact policy

Colony artifacts enter a private review queue. The Creator decides what reaches the public GitHub Wiki. The canonical ledger preserves rejected and quarantined artifacts without automatically publishing them.

## Compute constraint

Use local models wherever practical and optimize for near-zero API cost. Model calls remain behind typed citizen contracts and recorded outputs. The deterministic kernel and scripted replay must work without any model.

## Acceptance gates

1. Replay produces the same final state digest from seed plus ledger.
2. Every state transition maps to an event.
3. Energy conservation holds across all colony days.
4. Invalid capabilities fail without side effects.
5. Internal proposals cannot amend immutable articles.
6. Death is permanent at the identity level.
7. Every birth has a valid lineage and funded cost.
8. Scheduled and unscheduled Creator interventions are explicitly logged.
9. Public wiki artifacts require Creator approval and provenance.
10. Cultural-emergence claims separate observation from interpretation.
11. The world can be paused, resumed, forked, and destroyed without leaking outside its workspace.
12. Day 0 and Day 1 pass deterministically before hourly scheduling begins.
